# Finite-Cadence Quantum Metrology

**When can a finite-rate correction clock preserve Heisenberg precision, and how many cells does it take?** The project studies exact noisy sensing between boundary operations and measures precision by worst-case mean-square frequency error.

## Optimal correction budgets for commuting dephasing

For known commuting jumps, let $`\mathstrut{s}`$ be the least polynomial degree needed to express the signal on their joint spectrum, and set $`\mathstrut{m=\lceil s/2\rceil}`$. Every cell used for localization or measurement counts. With total sensing time $`\mathstrut{T}`$ and a common cell duration $`\mathstrut{h}`$, the optimal risk is

```math
R_h^*(T)=\Theta\!\left(\max\{T^{-2},\,h^{m-1}/T\}\right).
```

With a hard budget of $`\mathstrut{M}`$ cells and time $`\mathstrut{T}`$, arbitrary feedback-chosen intervals have optimal risk $`\mathstrut{\Theta(\max\{T^{-2},M^{-2+2/m}\})}`$ for $`\mathstrut{m\ge2}`$. The four-level cubic sensor needs order $`\mathstrut{T^2}`$ cells for inverse-square risk, while a six-level quintic sensor needs order $`\mathstrut{T^{3/2}}`$. Degree one or two gives the standard-quantum-limit order. The [correction-budget theorem](science/cadence_budget.md) gives both regimes, their hypotheses and the matching construction.

## When a fixed interval suffices

For a finite-dimensional sensor with Hermitian Lindblad noise, let $`\mathstrut{\mathcal A=C^*(I,L_1,\ldots,L_m)}`$ be the full algebra generated by the noise operators, and let $`\mathstrut{G}`$ generate the unknown signal. The relevant fixed-cadence criterion uses this entire algebra.

| Setting | Established consequence |
|---|---|
| $`\mathstrut{G\notin\mathcal A}`$ | A sufficiently small correction interval can be chosen once, independently of the sensing budget $`\mathstrut{T}`$, to attain global minimax mean-square error $`\mathstrut{\Theta(T^{-2})}`$. |
| $`\mathstrut{G\in\mathcal A}`$ | Every fixed positive interval has an $`\mathstrut{\Omega(T^{-1})}`$ error lower bound, even with adaptive boundary instruments and arbitrary noiseless ancillary memory, for each fixed bounded compensation range. |
| Four-level example $`\mathstrut{L=\mathrm{diag}(-3,-1,1,3)}`$, $`\mathstrut{G=L^3}`$ | Arbitrarily fast correction permits global $`\mathstrut{\Theta(T^{-2})}`$ error, but every positive fixed clock obeys the preceding lower bound. |
| Transverse qubit with known positive noise rate and compensation-grid spacing $`\mathstrut{\Delta}`$ | In the stated small-band regime, the optimal error is $`\mathstrut{\Theta(T^{-2}+\tau\Delta^2/T)}`$, uniformly over grid offsets and $`\mathstrut{0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\le W}`$. |

The lower bound in the second row does not assert that the standard quantum limit is attainable when the parameter is unidentifiable. In the first row the interval is **chosen sufficiently small**, rather than supplied arbitrarily. Every trial, including an unsuccessful localization trial, is charged to a deterministic total sensing-time budget.

## Learn the project

The educational anchor is Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac L. Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*, **10th Anniversary Edition (2010)**. Its treatment of quantum operations, distinguishability and error correction supplies the foundation; the finite-cadence sensing and global estimation arguments are developed here.

## Reproduce the finite checks

From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r verification/requirements.txt
python3 verification/run_all.py
```

The suite checks noncommuting algebra recovery and exact channel derivatives, the transverse-qubit response, grid-estimator margins, physical Choi bounds, the four-level fast protocol, and polynomial-syndrome and channel-information calculations for the correction-budget law. It writes numerical results under `verification/results/`. Finite matrix computations support their tested cases; the general and asymptotic statements depend on the analytical proofs.

## Scope

The model assumes parameter-independent Hermitian Markovian noise, a known signal generator, ideal instantaneous boundary operations, noiseless ancillas and a calibrated physical compensation field. There is one active sensing system and no access to its environment, no subinterval control and no quantum-coherent control register for the field. The general positive theorem tolerates unknown bounded rates along known noise directions. The correction-budget law uses known commuting dephasing. The grid upper bound assumes the transverse noise rate is known and positive. Unknown field bias, faulty recovery, noisy ancillas and general non-Hermitian jumps are outside these guarantees.
