# How many correction cells does sensing need?

[Model and other results](model.md) · [Attaining the bounds](cadence_budget_protocol.md) · [Adaptive lower bounds](cadence_budget_converse.md)

The four-level example shows that a fixed positive correction interval can destroy inverse-square precision. The correction-budget law determines how fast the clock must run. The answer depends on how many factors of the noise operators are needed to reproduce the signal on the sensor's spectrum.

## Model

Use one finite-dimensional sensor with known mutually commuting Hermitian Lindblad jumps $`\mathstrut{L_1,\ldots,L_r}`$. Include their known strengths in those operators. For a cell of duration $`\mathstrut{h}`$ the sensor undergoes the exact channel

```math
\Phi_{h,\omega}
=\exp\!\left[h\left(\mathcal D-i\omega[G,\,\cdot]\right)\right],
\qquad
\mathcal D(X)=\sum_j\left(L_j XL_j-\tfrac12\{L_j^2,X\}\right).
```

The unknown frequency $`\mathstrut{\omega}`$ lies in one fixed band $`\mathstrut{[-W,W]}`$, with $`\mathstrut{W\gt 0}`$. The known, nonscalar Hermitian $`\mathstrut{G}`$ belongs to the algebra generated by the jumps. Equivalently, it is a real function on their distinct joint eigenvalue tuples, constant within each simultaneous eigenspace. Write $`\mathstrut{\mathcal A_d}`$ for the space of real polynomials in the jumps of total degree at most $`\mathstrut{d}`$, evaluated as sensor operators. Define $`\mathstrut{s}`$ as the least $`\mathstrut{d}`$ with $`\mathstrut{G\in\mathcal A_d}`$, and set $`\mathstrut{m=\lceil s/2\rceil}`$. This degree refers to the finite spectrum. A high formal power can have smaller degree if it coincides with a lower polynomial on that spectrum.

Every used cell counts, including cells spent on calibration, localization or a discarded trial. Between cells the controller can prepare states, apply recoveries, measure, retain noiseless quantum memory and use the measurement record. These boundary operations are ideal and take zero sensing time. One sensor evolves during each cell. There is no access to the environment and no control within a cell. The variable-duration model permits classical feedback on cell durations and stopping, with the budgets obeyed on every history. Since $`\mathstrut{G}`$ commutes with the jumps, a known detuning rotation at a boundary exactly implements a constant counterfield for that cell. A scalar $`\mathstrut{G}`$ carries no frequency information.

## Uniform-interval law

Write $`\mathstrut{R_h^*(T)}`$ for the infimum, over allowed protocols using cells of common length $`\mathstrut{h}`$ with total sensing time at most $`\mathstrut{T}`$, of the worst-case mean-square frequency error on the band. There are positive $`\mathstrut{h_0,T_0}`$ and positive constants that depend only on the fixed sensor and band such that, uniformly for $`\mathstrut{0\lt h\le h_0}`$ and $`\mathstrut{T\ge T_0}`$,

```math
R_h^*(T)=\Theta\!\left(\max\{T^{-2},\,h^{m-1}/T\}\right).
```

For $`\mathstrut{m=1}`$ the order is $`\mathstrut{T^{-1}}`$. When $`\mathstrut{m\ge2}`$, preserving the $`\mathstrut{T^{-2}}`$ law requires and admits $`\mathstrut{h=O(T^{-1/(m-1)})}`$. A finite sensing run of duration comparable to $`\mathstrut{T}`$ therefore needs order $`\mathstrut{T^{m/(m-1)}}`$ cells at the least demanding uniform cadence.

## Time and cell budgets with feedback

Write $`\mathstrut{R^*(T,M)}`$ for the corresponding optimal risk when the controller may vary durations, with a hard cap of $`\mathstrut{M}`$ cells and total sensing time at most $`\mathstrut{T}`$ on every history. For sufficiently large $`\mathstrut{T}`$ and $`\mathstrut{M}`$, uniformly in their relative size,

```math
R^*(T,M)=
\begin{cases}
\Theta\!\left(\max\{T^{-1},M^{-1}\}\right),& m=1,\\
\Theta\!\left(\max\{T^{-2},M^{-2+2/m}\}\right),& m\ge2.
\end{cases}
```

For $`\mathstrut{m\ge2}`$ the minimum cell budget for inverse-square precision has order $`\mathstrut{T^{m/(m-1)}}`$, even with irregular, feedback-chosen intervals. A uniform schedule using at most $`\mathstrut{M}`$ cells attains the order. For a tight cell budget it can finish before $`\mathstrut{T}`$. This is a bound on the number of sensing intervals separated by available boundary interventions.

| Signal and spectrum | Least degree | Order of the cell budget for inverse-square risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cubic signal $`\mathstrut{G=L^3}`$ on four distinct eigenvalues of $`\mathstrut{L}`$ | 3 | $`\mathstrut{T^2}`$ |
| Quintic signal $`\mathstrut{G=L^5}`$ on six distinct eigenvalues of $`\mathstrut{L}`$ | 5 | $`\mathstrut{T^{3/2}}`$ |

On these spectra a lower-degree polynomial cannot agree with the indicated powers: their difference would be a nonzero polynomial with more roots than its degree. The cubic row makes the sufficient cell count from the [original four-level construction](fast_fixed_separation.md) sharp. The quintic row shows how a signal can distinguish the codewords while the code corrects more orders of noise.

## Why total degree fixes the crossover

Correcting all noise words through degree $`\mathstrut{m-1}`$ equates the codeword moments through degree $`\mathstrut{2m-2}`$. The signal has higher degree, so a projected signal gap survives. The first possible residual logical loss is of order $`\mathstrut{h^m}`$ per cell. Its accumulation limits reliable Ramsey interrogations to times of order $`\mathstrut{h^{1-m}}`$. Localizing to that scale and repeating the calibrated readout yields the uniform upper bound.

The lower bound works directly with the full noisy cell. The channel-extension representation can split any degree-$`\mathstrut{a}`$ signal monomial across two Kraus amplitudes of degrees as close as possible to $`\mathstrut{a/2}`$. This controls how quickly the per-cell information bound can diverge as $`\mathstrut{h}`$ decreases. For a variable schedule, a single cutoff bounds the total coherent contribution of the short cells and the sum of the information contributions from the long cells. Balancing them gives the same cell-count exponent as the construction.

The theorem concerns commuting Markovian dephasing, known rates and ideal recovery.
