# Model, assumptions and results

[Repository overview](../README.md) · [Physical interpretation](interpretation.md)

## The question

Infinitesimal error correction probes which Hamiltonian components survive the leading error operators. A correction interval of positive duration contains trajectories with arbitrarily many jumps. Their products can generate a larger error algebra. We ask whether adaptive physical compensation can retain a global Heisenberg error law at one fixed correction rate, and when every strategy at that rate must fail to do so.

“Global” means a uniform mean-square-error guarantee on a fixed parameter band, without a shrinking accurate prior supplied free of charge. “Fixed cadence” means the positive interval is independent of the final sensing budget, not that it is large or experimentally imposed.

## Exact sensing cells

The sensor Hilbert space has finite dimension $`d`$. The signal generator $`G=G^\dagger`$ and noise directions are known. For fixed Hermitian operators $`L_j=L_j^\dagger`$, define

```math
\mathcal D(\rho)=\sum_j\left(L_j\rho L_j-\frac12\{L_j^2,\rho\}\right),
\qquad
\mathcal E_{\omega,c}^{(\tau)}
=\exp\!\left[\tau\bigl(\mathcal D-i(\omega-c)[G,\cdot]\bigr)\right].
```

The unknown real parameter lies in $`\omega\in[-W,W]`$, where $`W>0`$ is fixed. A known additional Hamiltonian drift is assumed physically canceled; an unknown drift is not included. The calibrated field $`-cG`$ acts simultaneously with the noise and signal throughout a cell. It is constant within the cell and can be changed at a boundary using the previous classical history.

The physical channel is the exponential of the sum of generators. It is generally **not** the product of a noise channel and a separate signal unitary. A known rotation after the cell therefore cannot generally replace the in-cell compensation.

### Allowed operations and resources

- Exactly one active sensor evolves during each cell. Noiseless ancillas may accompany it; their within-cell evolution is the identity.
- Arbitrary parameter-independent quantum instruments, state preparation, recovery and classical processing are allowed at cell boundaries. These ideal boundary operations take no sensing time.
- The controller may retain the complete classical record and quantum memory. At a fixed record its chosen field and instrument are independent of the unknown parameter.
- There is no access to the environment or jump record, no quantum operation inside a cell, and no coherently controlled field register.
- For the general fixed-cadence converse, the allowed classical field range is any fixed bounded interval $`[-C,C]`$. The constructive protocol uses centers inside the original parameter band, so $`C=W`$ suffices.
- With a hard total sensing-time budget $`T`$, at most $`N=\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor`$ complete cells are available. All repeated measurements, preparation failures and wrong localization decisions consume their actual cells. Stopping and unused remaining time cannot increase this budget.

Preparation and recovery apparatus can use reset ancillary degrees of freedom at boundaries. The construction’s persistent coherent encoding ancilla has dimension at most $`d`$; that dimension statement does not count a measuring/resetting apparatus as absent.

For a protocol $`\mathsf P`$ and its estimator, define

```math
R_T(\mathsf P)=\sup_{\omega\in[-W,W]}
\mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\omega-\omega)^2],
\qquad
R_T^*=\inf_{\mathsf P}R_T(\mathsf P).
```

The expectation includes all classical outcomes. Estimators need not be unbiased. Constants in asymptotic notation may depend on the fixed physical model, band and cadence; any additional uniformity is stated explicitly.

## The full noise algebra

Let

```math
\mathcal A=C^*(I,L_1,\ldots,L_m)
```

be the finite-dimensional unital complex star algebra generated by the **actual** jumps. Since the generators are Hermitian, it is the linear span of all their finite words, including the identity. Hilbert–Schmidt projection onto this space is denoted $`\Pi_{\mathcal A}`$.

The familiar fast-control Lindblad span, for Hermitian jumps, is the real Hermitian space

```math
\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\left\{I,L_j,\frac{\{L_j,L_k\}}2,
\frac{[L_j,L_k]}{2i}\right\}.
```

It is contained in the Hermitian part of $`\mathcal A`$ and can be strictly smaller. The distinction is explicit in the [four-level example](fast_fixed_separation.md).

## Result 1: the fixed-cadence criterion

**Positive direction.** If $`G\notin\mathcal A`$, there is a sufficiently small $`\tau>0`$, chosen once from the fixed model and prior, a fixed code and recovery, and a sequence of adaptive protocols satisfying

```math
R_T^*=\Theta(T^{-2})
```

as $`T\to\infty`$. The protocol has a persistent noiseless encoding ancilla of dimension at most $`d`$. Its quantum recovery and readout observables do not acquire increasing parameter precision. The classical compensation values do acquire increasing accuracy. The proof gives a deterministic time bound and covers the risk of all wrong decisions.

The [constructive proof](constructive_proof.md) identifies the residual $`Q=G-\Pi_{\mathcal A}G`$, constructs an explicit decoder correcting every channel with Kraus operators in the algebra, and proves the useful exact-cell Hamiltonian tangent

```math
B_\tau=\int_0^\tau e^{t\mathcal D^\dagger}(G)\,dt.
```

For Hermitian noise the signal residual has strictly positive overlap with this integrated tangent. A quadratic detuning remainder and overlapping localization intervals turn that local fact into the global guarantee.

**Negative direction.** If $`G\in\mathcal A`$, then for every fixed $`\tau>0`$ and every fixed bounded compensation range, there is a model-dependent constant $`M<\infty`$ such that every allowed strategy satisfies

```math
R_T(\mathsf P)\ge
\frac{1}{\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor M+\pi^2/W^2}.
```

In particular the minimax risk is $`\Omega(T^{-1})`$. The proof applies to arbitrary boundary codes, quantum memory, adaptive fields and measurements, rather than only to the constructive decoder. It proves exact trajectory Kraus span $`\mathcal A`$, then controls information growth using the channel right-logarithmic-derivative bound and a Bayesian lower bound. See the [converse](converse_proof.md).

The negative result is a lower bound. A scalar $`G`$, or a band containing aliases of the exact cell channel, can prevent even consistent estimation. No universal $`\Theta(T^{-1})`$ attainment statement is made.

Together, these directions classify whether some sufficiently small, horizon-independent cadence can attain the global Heisenberg law under the stated controls. They do not promise the law at every externally supplied cadence.

## Unknown rates along known directions

For the positive result one may instead know Hermitian directions $`F_j`$ and upper bounds $`\Gamma_j<\infty`$, while

```math
L_j=\sqrt{\gamma_j}F_j,\qquad
0\le\gamma_j\le\Gamma_j
```

are fixed but unknown throughout the experiment. Define the envelope algebra

```math
\mathcal A_F=C^*(I,F_1,\ldots,F_m).
```

If $`G\notin\mathcal A_F`$, one code, recovery, cadence and estimator family give the positive bound uniformly over this rate box. The decoder is independent of the rates, and the global sign test requires only known bounds on the logical slope. This is a sufficient robust statement.

If a rate vanishes, the actual algebra $`\mathcal A_\gamma`$ may shrink. Membership in the envelope algebra is therefore not a necessity statement for every realization. The general converse is applied to the actual jumps. Its constant need not remain bounded as a positive rate approaches zero. A minimax lower bound over unknown rates can fix one allowed difficult realization, but that is distinct from a uniform lower bound for each rate.

## Result 2: a strict fast-versus-fixed separation

For one four-level sensor set

```math
L=\operatorname{diag}(-3,-1,1,3),\qquad G=L^3.
```

Here $`G\notin\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,L,L^2\}`$, while $`G\in C^*(I,L)`$. An explicit two-dimensional code admits a global fast-correction protocol with error $`\Theta(T^{-2})`$ under the same hard sensing-time accounting. In that construction the interval $`h(T)=\Theta(T^{-1})`$ and the number of corrections is $`\Theta(T^2)`$. This is a resource statement about the construction, not a lower bound on every possible fast protocol.

Every positive fixed interval instead obeys the SQL lower bound. For this commuting example the lower bound is even uniform over unbounded classical compensation values. On an identifiable interval shorter than $`\pi/\tau`$, a two-quadrature protocol attains $`O(T^{-1})`$; bands containing an alias pair can have constant risk. The [separation proof](fast_fixed_separation.md) establishes both global comparisons and the alias period.

## Result 3: a compensation-resolution law

For the transverse qubit take

```math
G=Z/2,\qquad L=\sqrt\gamma X,\qquad \gamma>0.
```

The grid upper bound assumes known $`\gamma`$. Fix a sufficiently small positive $`\tau`$ and a band $`[-W,W]`$ satisfying the explicit small-band conditions in the [upper proof](grid_upper.md). Allowed field values lie on a uniform grid of spacing $`\Delta`$ and arbitrary offset; nearest-grid values for centers in the band must be available. Fix $`0<\Delta_0\le W`$ compatible with those conditions. Uniformly over offsets and $`0<\Delta\le\Delta_0`$,

```math
R_T^*=\Theta\!\left(T^{-2}+\frac{\tau\Delta^2}{T}\right).
```

The constants are independent of the grid spacing and offset, so the law also applies to a prescribed spacing $`\Delta(T)`$ in this range. The upper proof uses a one-qubit noiseless ancilla, fixed parity recovery, fixed X/Y readouts and classical processing of their sample means. The [lower proof](grid_lower.md) acts on the unrecovered physical channel and allows arbitrary boundary instruments and memory, including changing grid values after every cell. It does not assume the candidate recovery.

The restriction $`\Delta_0\le W`$ ensures an interior lattice gap for every offset in the matching lower bound. The upper construction alone covers some larger grids. With fixed $`\tau`$, retaining Heisenberg order requires $`\Delta=O(T^{-1/2})`$; dimensionally, the comparison is $`\tau\Delta^2\lesssim T^{-1}`$. Fixed nonzero spacing eventually produces the $`T^{-1}`$ term.

The known-positive-rate grid result is not a theorem for unknown rates. At zero noise the particular grid obstruction disappears.

## Boundaries and proof dependencies

The [related-work account](../references/RELATED_WORK.md) credits algebraic QEC, fast HNLS, local channel-metrology criteria, RLD chain rules, exact transverse responses and earlier adaptive-estimation ideas. The present claims use these inputs with the exact physical cell and a complete global budget argument.

The model does not include imperfect recovery, a noisy ancilla, uncertain signal direction, unknown calibration bias, non-Hermitian jumps, non-Markovian memory, access to a detected jump record, subcell pulse sequences or a quantum-coherent compensation register. Continuous real-valued compensation is an explicit resource in the first theorem; its finite resolution is analyzed separately in the grid witness.

The [verification suite](../verification/README.md) checks selected finite instances. It does not establish the universal trajectory-support, asymptotic minimax or infinite-horizon claims by sampling.
