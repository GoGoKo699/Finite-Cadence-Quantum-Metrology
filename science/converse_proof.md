# Why a fixed clock fails when the signal belongs to the noise algebra

This page proves the lower-bound direction of the [model and results](model.md).
It concerns the physical sensing channel before any recovery. The result
therefore applies to arbitrary boundary codes, coherent ancillary memory,
adaptive measurements and classical compensation policies.

The channel RLD formula and its amortized chain rule are inherited from
[Katariya and Wilde, Proposition 29 and Proposition 38](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10708).
The finite-time Kraus-support calculation below supplies their hypothesis for
this physical model. See [the related-work comparison](../references/RELATED_WORK.md)
for the distinction between these inherited tools and the operational result.

## 1. Statement and quantifiers

Let the sensor have dimension $`\mathstrut{d\lt \infty}`$, with known Hermitian signal generator
$`\mathstrut{G}`$ and parameter-independent Hermitian jump operators $`\mathstrut{L_1,\ldots,L_r}`$.
Absorb the actual fixed jump rates into the $`\mathstrut{L_j}`$, and write

```math
\mathcal D(\rho)=\sum_j\left(L_j\rho L_j-
\frac12\{L_j^2,\rho\}\right),\qquad
\mathcal A=C^*(I,L_1,\ldots,L_r).
```

The frequency belongs to the fixed band $`\mathstrut{[-W,W]}`$, where $`\mathstrut{W>0}`$. During each
cell, a calibrated classical field $`\mathstrut{c\in[-C,C]}`$ is held constant and the
joint sensor–ancilla channel is

```math
\mathcal E_{\omega-c}^{(\tau)}\otimes\mathrm{id}_A,
\qquad
\mathcal E_\delta^{(\tau)}=
\exp\!\left[\tau\big(\mathcal D-i\delta[G,\cdot]\big)\right].
```

Both the cell length $`\mathstrut{\tau>0}`$ and field bound $`\mathstrut{C\lt \infty}`$ are independent of
the sensing budget $`\mathstrut{T}`$. At cell boundaries the protocol may apply any
parameter-independent quantum instrument, retain noiseless quantum and
classical memory, and choose the next $`\mathstrut{c}`$ from its record. The environment
is inaccessible, and no control acts inside a cell except the stated
constant physical field. The hard budget allows at most
$`\mathstrut{N=\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor}`$ cells on every history, including failed trials.

**Theorem.** If $`\mathstrut{G\in\mathcal A}`$, there is a finite, model-dependent constant
$`\mathstrut{M}`$ such that every allowed protocol satisfies

```math
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{data}}(\omega)\le NM,\qquad \omega\in[-W,W],
\end{aligned}
\tag{C1}
```

and its minimax mean-squared error obeys

```math
\begin{aligned}
R_T:=\inf_{\mathsf P}\sup_{\omega\in[-W,W]}
\mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\omega-\omega)^2
\ \ge\ \frac{1}{NM+\pi^2/W^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C2}
```

In particular $`\mathstrut{R_T=\Omega(T^{-1})}`$. This is a lower bound, not a general
SQL attainment theorem. A scalar signal or an aliased frequency band can
prevent consistent estimation. The [four-level example](fast_fixed_separation.md)
provides both an alias-free SQL upper bound and a strict contrast with a
clock that becomes faster as $`\mathstrut{T}`$ grows.

The algebra in this theorem is generated by the **actual** jumps. If
$`\mathstrut{L_j=\sqrt{\gamma_j}F_j}`$ with possibly zero rates, it can be smaller than
$`\mathstrut{C^*(I,F_1,\ldots,F_r)}`$. Membership in that larger envelope alone does not
prove this lower bound at a realization where some rates vanish. Nor is
the constant $`\mathstrut{M}`$ claimed uniform as positive rates approach zero.

## 2. Exact finite-time Kraus support

Set

```math
K_\delta=-i\delta G-\frac12\sum_j L_j^2.
```

The completely positive Dyson expansion of the semigroup sums over all
jump counts $`\mathstrut{m\ge0}`$, labels $`\mathstrut{j_1,\ldots,j_m}`$, and ordered times
$`\mathstrut{0\lt t_1\lt \cdots\lt t_m\lt \tau}`$. Its trajectory Kraus operator is

```math
\begin{aligned}
V_{\delta,\boldsymbol j,\boldsymbol t}
=e^{K_\delta(\tau-t_m)}L_{j_m}
 e^{K_\delta(t_m-t_{m-1})}\cdots
 L_{j_1}e^{K_\delta t_1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C3}
```

The no-jump term is $`\mathstrut{e^{K_\delta\tau}}`$. The expansion converges in finite
dimension. It expresses the Choi operator as a positive sum of integrals
of rank-one trajectory projectors; no monitoring of the physical
environment is assumed by using this representation.

Use output-first vectorization

```math
|X\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{a,b}X_{ab}
|a\rangle_{\mathrm{out}}|b\rangle_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad
J_\delta=(\mathcal E_\delta^{(\tau)}\otimes\mathrm{id})
(|\Phi_d\rangle\langle\Phi_d|),\quad
|\Phi_d\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_a|aa\rangle.
```

Let $`\mathstrut{\mathcal V_\delta}`$ be the operator subspace corresponding to
$`\mathstrut{\mathrm{supp}J_\delta}`$. For any vector in the kernel of $`\mathstrut{J_\delta}`$,
positivity of the trajectory integral implies orthogonality to each
trajectory for almost every point of its open time simplex. A trajectory
is continuous in its times. A continuous scalar function that vanishes
almost everywhere on the simplex vanishes everywhere there and on all
boundary limits. Thus every such limit belongs to the Choi support.
This argument also explains why a particular choice of continuous Kraus
representation causes no ambiguity: the support is intrinsic to the
channel.

If $`\mathstrut{G\in\mathcal A}`$, then $`\mathstrut{K_\delta\in\mathcal A}`$. Every exponential and
product in (C3) lies in $`\mathstrut{\mathcal A}`$, so

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V_\delta\subseteq\mathcal A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C4}
```

For the reverse inclusion, fix any word $`\mathstrut{L_{j_m}\cdots L_{j_1}}`$ and take
all its jump times to $`\mathstrut{\tau}`$ from below, retaining their order. Equation
(C3) tends to

```math
L_{j_m}\cdots L_{j_1}e^{K_\delta\tau}.
```

The support argument places this limit in $`\mathstrut{\mathcal V_\delta}`$. Because
the generators are Hermitian, the complex span of all finite words,
including the empty word, is exactly the unital star algebra $`\mathstrut{\mathcal A}`$.
It is already closed in finite dimension. Consequently

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A e^{K_\delta\tau}\subseteq\mathcal V_\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C5}
```

The exponential is invertible, with $`\mathstrut{e^{-K_\delta\tau}\in\mathcal A}`$.
Right multiplication by $`\mathstrut{e^{K_\delta\tau}}`$ is therefore a bijection on
$`\mathstrut{\mathcal A}`$. Combining (C4) and (C5) proves

```math
\begin{aligned}
\boxed{\mathcal V_\delta=\mathcal A\quad
\text{for every real }\delta.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{C6}
```

No positive probability has been assigned to simultaneous jumps or to a
jump exactly at a boundary. Only continuity and the closed support of a
positive integral were used. Hermiticity is material here: for general
non-Hermitian jumps the generated word space need not equal the full star
algebra, so this proof cannot simply be reused with the same criterion.

## 3. A uniformly finite channel information bound

Let $`\mathstrut{P_{\mathcal A}}`$ denote the orthogonal projection onto the vectorized
space $`\mathstrut{\mathcal A}`$. Equation (C6) gives a support independent of detuning:

```math
J_\delta=P_{\mathcal A}J_\delta P_{\mathcal A},\qquad
J_\delta\big|_{\mathcal A}>0.
```

The matrix is analytic in $`\mathstrut{\delta}`$. Compactness of the accessible interval
$`\mathstrut{\mathcal K=[-W-C,W+C]}`$ therefore gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_*:=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal K}
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(J_\delta\big|_{\mathcal A}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C7}
```

The fixed support also gives
$`\mathstrut{\dot J_\delta=P_{\mathcal A}\dot J_\delta P_{\mathcal A}}`$, where the dot
means differentiation with respect to $`\mathstrut{\delta}`$. Ambient full rank is
unnecessary: a proper noise algebra gives a proper but fixed support.

Write $`\mathstrut{D_G=\lambda_{\max}(G)-\lambda_{\min}(G)}`$. Subtracting the midpoint
of the spectrum from $`\mathstrut{G}`$ does not change its commutator and yields

```math
\|-i[G,\cdot]\|_\diamond\le D_G.
```

Duhamel differentiation gives an integral of this commutator between two
channels. Diamond-norm contraction and the normalized Choi input imply

```math
\begin{aligned}
\|\dot J_\delta\|_1\le\tau D_G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C8}
```

For the unnormalized Choi operator $`\mathstrut{\Gamma_\delta=dJ_\delta}`$, the channel
right-logarithmic-derivative (RLD) Fisher information is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\widehat I(\mathcal E_\delta^{(\tau)})
=\left\|\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{out}}
\left[\dot\Gamma_\delta\Gamma_\delta^+\dot\Gamma_\delta\right]
\right\|_\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C9}
```

Here $`\mathstrut{+}`$ means the inverse on the support, extended by zero. The support
condition established above is exactly what makes this expression finite.
Equation (C9) is Proposition 29, Eq. (5.151), in
[Katariya and Wilde](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10708).
The operator inside the partial trace is positive, so its operator norm
is bounded by its trace. Thus

```math
\begin{aligned}
\widehat I(\mathcal E_\delta^{(\tau)})
&\le d\mathrm{Tr}(\dot J_\delta J_\delta^+\dot J_\delta)\\
&\le \frac{d}{\lambda_*}\mathrm{Tr}(\dot J_\delta^2)\\
&\le \frac{d\tau^2D_G^2}{\lambda_*}
=:M\lt \infty,\qquad \delta\in\mathcal K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C10}
```

The bound is deliberately nonoptimal. Its role is to be finite and
uniform over every permitted field choice and frequency. It does not
remain controlled merely by letting $`\mathstrut{C}`$ grow with $`\mathstrut{T}`$; that would change
the compact set in (C7). The [commuting four-level example](fast_fixed_separation.md)
has a stronger, detuning-independent bound without any field-amplitude
restriction.

## 4. Adaptive histories and coherent memory are included

For a differentiable state family with the requisite support condition,
write its RLD information as

```math
\widehat I(\rho_\omega)=
\mathrm{Tr}(\dot\rho_\omega\rho_\omega^+\dot\rho_\omega).
```

The channel chain rule of Katariya and Wilde, Proposition 38,
Eq. (5.222), states

```math
\begin{aligned}
\widehat I\!\left[(\mathcal E_\omega\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)
(\rho_\omega)\right]
\le \widehat I(\mathcal E_\omega)+\widehat I(\rho_\omega).
\end{aligned}
\tag{C11}
```

It permits arbitrary ancillary memory and already accounts for
parameter dependence present in the input. RLD information also obeys
data processing under a parameter-independent channel or instrument when
the complete output record is retained.

To apply this to feedback, retain the full classical history $`\mathstrut{h}`$ and the
remaining quantum memory in the state

```math
\sigma_\omega=
\bigoplus_h p_\omega(h)\rho_{\omega,h}.
```

Direct differentiation of each block gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\widehat I(\sigma_\omega)
=I(p_\omega)+
\sum_h p_\omega(h)\widehat I(\rho_{\omega,h}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{C12}
```

Indeed the cross term is proportional to
$`\mathstrut{\mathrm{Tr}\dot\rho_{\omega,h}=0}`$. Finite RLD information ensures
that the derivative is supported on the state support, which justifies
this cancellation also for a singular conditional state. Zero-probability
branches are handled by the usual limiting convention. Integrals replace
sums for a continuous record with its dominating measure; no discretized
controller precision is assumed.

At a fixed history, the policy chooses a number $`\mathstrut{c(h)}`$. When
differentiating that branch, $`\mathstrut{c(h)}`$ is fixed, so the channel derivative
is $`\mathstrut{\partial_\delta\mathcal E_\delta}`$ at $`\mathstrut{\delta=\omega-c(h)}`$. The
parameter dependence of the probability of reaching $`\mathstrut{h}`$ has not been
discarded: it is the first term in (C12). Therefore (C10) and (C11)
increase the full history-plus-memory information by at most $`\mathstrut{M}`$ per
cell. The next boundary instrument cannot increase it. This argument
allows discontinuous decision rules as functions of the observed history;
it does not differentiate the policy with respect to the unknown parameter.

The initial state and any random seed are parameter independent and have
zero information. Iterating proves an RLD bound $`\mathstrut{NM}`$ on the full final
state. The final measurement is another parameter-independent operation;
on a classical output RLD is ordinary Fisher information. Equation (C1)
follows. There is no dimension cap on the finite ancillary system used by
the protocol, and it may grow with $`\mathstrut{N}`$.

An early-stopping protocol with at most $`\mathstrut{N}`$ cells can be padded by dummy
cells on discarded systems, retaining its original output unchanged.
Postselection likewise retains both success and failure in the complete
record and charges all used cells. Neither changes (C1).

## 5. Global risk without an unbiasedness assumption

Choose the smooth prior

```math
\pi_W(\omega)=\frac1W
\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi\omega}{2W}\right),
\qquad -W\le\omega\le W.
```

It integrates to one, vanishes at both endpoints, and has prior Fisher
information

```math
\begin{aligned}
I(\pi_W)=\int_{-W}^{W}
\frac{[\pi_W'(\omega)]^2}{\pi_W(\omega)}\,d\omega
=\frac{\pi^2}{W^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C13}
```

Let $`\mathstrut{q(\omega,y)=\pi_W(\omega)p_\omega(y)}`$ be the joint distribution of
parameter and complete data, and $`\mathstrut{s=\partial_\omega\log q}`$ its score.
The estimator may be clipped to the prior band without increasing risk.
Integration by parts, using the zero prior at the endpoints, gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_q[(\widehat\omega(y)-\omega)s(\omega,y)]=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C14}
```

The conditional data score has mean zero. Hence

```math
\mathbb E_q s^2
=\mathbb E_{\pi_W}I_{\mathrm{data}}(\omega)+I(\pi_W)
\le NM+\frac{\pi^2}{W^2}.
```

Cauchy–Schwarz applied to (C14) yields the corresponding Bayes risk bound
(C2); minimax risk is at least Bayes risk. The argument requires no
unbiasedness and applies to the globally fixed frequency band. If the
left-hand risk or score is undefined through a singular statistical model,
the usual regularization/limiting form gives the same bound; the analytic
finite-dimensional channel family here supplies the relevant smooth
dominated experiment.

## 6. Consequence and boundaries

At positive time, the physical channel samples every finite word in the
Hermitian noise operators. When the signal is in their full algebra,
its Choi derivative remains inside one fixed, strictly positive support.
That limits the information added per fixed-duration cell even when the
controller keeps the entire history and quantum memory.

Together with the [constructive proof](constructive_proof.md), this gives
an operational classification: a sufficiently small cadence chosen once
can support global $`\mathstrut{T^{-2}}`$ risk when $`\mathstrut{G\notin\mathcal A}`$, whereas no
fixed positive cadence supports that order when $`\mathstrut{G\in\mathcal A}`$ under
the stated bounded-field model. The positive statement is an existence
claim for a suitable cadence, not a claim about every supplied clock.

The argument does not cover non-Hermitian jumps by replacement of symbols,
unknown Hamiltonian drift, noise whose generators change with the control,
subcell interventions, access to a jump record, or coherently controlled
field amplitudes. Such changes alter the physical channel family used in
the support and information calculation.
