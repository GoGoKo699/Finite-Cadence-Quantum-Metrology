# Related work and the scope of the contribution

[Primary sources](SOURCES.md) · [Model and results](../science/model.md) · [Physical interpretation](../science/interpretation.md)

The question here is whether one positive correction interval, chosen once from the physical model and a fixed frequency band, can support uniform inverse-square mean squared error as the total sensing time grows. The controlled channel is the exact simultaneous Lindblad evolution, with a calibrated classical counterfield held throughout each cell. Every measurement batch and every failed branch is included in the sensing budget. The [model](../science/model.md) states the precise hypotheses and quantifiers.

Algebraic error correction, cancellation near an estimated parameter, local channel criteria, global phase estimation, and channel-information bounds all have substantial precedents. The contribution is the fixed-cadence operational classification and the matched compensation-grid law within this specific physical access model. The claims do not include a new general principle of correction outside a noise algebra or a new generic noise-to-precision exponent.

## Fast correction and one fixed clock

For Hermitian jumps, the usual infinitesimal Lindblad span is the real space

```math
\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\left\{I,L_j,\frac{L_jL_k+L_kL_j}{2},
\frac{L_jL_k-L_kL_j}{2i}\right\}.
```

It lies in the Hermitian part of the full complex noise algebra
$`\mathcal A=C^*(I,L_1,\ldots,L_r)`$. The distinction is between the degree-two span and all finite products, with the real and complex spaces kept explicit. [HNLS18](SOURCES.md#hnls18) supplies the fast-control criterion. The four-level example in [Fast versus fixed correction](../science/fast_fixed_separation.md) has $`G=L^3\notin\mathcal S`$ but $`G\in\mathcal A`$. Its global fast protocol and fixed-clock converse make the different order of limits concrete.

[FAST17](SOURCES.md#fast17) already establishes global inverse-square risk, with sensing time charged, under full and fast control. Thus global estimation itself is inherited. [ADAPT17](SOURCES.md#adapt17) and [HNLS18](SOURCES.md#hnls18) also discuss cancellation around a working point. At finite duration, a physical counter-Hamiltonian present during noisy evolution can differ from a unitary applied only after the cell. That distinction matters when signal and noise do not commute.

## Local channel criteria and generated noise algebras

[CHANNEL21](SOURCES.md#channel21), Theorems 1–3, classifies local asymptotic channel QFI through the Hamiltonian-not-in-Kraus-span condition and supplies corrected channels with a useful noiseless tangent. The local part of the present construction uses this established geometry. The [constructive proof](../science/constructive_proof.md) explicitly computes the finite-cell tangent and then supplies one global estimator, rather than optimizing a different local experiment at each unknown frequency.

[SUBSYSTEM26](SOURCES.md#subsystem26), Eq. (1), assumes factorized Pauli-error Kraus operators $`K_i^\theta=\sqrt{p_i}E_i e^{-i\theta H}`$. Theorem 2 already gives Heisenberg scaling when the signal lies outside the algebra generated by the Pauli errors, using subsystem error correction. [ANCILLA25](SOURCES.md#ancilla25), Theorem 3, gives an earlier ancilla-free Pauli result with an additional commutation condition. These are direct antecedents for algebraic sufficiency. The additional statements proved here concern the exact simultaneous semigroup, a once-chosen physical clock, fully charged global attainability, and an all-adaptive fixed-clock obstruction.

In the unknown-rate extension, the code corrects the algebra generated by the known noise directions. This gives a uniform sufficient condition over bounded nonnegative rates. The converse uses the algebra of the actual jumps. A direction with zero rate can disappear from that algebra, so the envelope condition is not asserted to be necessary for every rate realization.

## Finite-rate response and adaptive compensation

[FIXED21](SOURCES.md#fixed21), Eq. (6), already gives the transverse-noise finite-cycle coherence used in the qubit example, up to conjugation conventions. Section 4 explicitly considers adaptive inverse-Hamiltonian ideas and the remaining phase-spread problem. The present global construction does not dispute its fixed-detuning formulas or recover the noiseless leading constant. It proves an inverse-square risk order through repeated, data-driven physical retuning at a fixed correction interval.

[BIAS22](SOURCES.md#bias22) emphasizes that finite-rate correction also changes the signal response. The grid protocol therefore uses the calibrated nonlinear phase of the exact response. A postulated unmodified phase slope would be insufficient for refinement below the field spacing.

Linear sensitivity with quadratic loss in the estimation mismatch is also prior work. Published Eq. (12) in [INFORMATIVE](SOURCES.md#informative), and Eqs. (14)–(15) and the adaptive simulations in its earlier 2020 manuscript, exhibit that mechanism for parameter-dependent noise under fast correction. The two versions have different titles and author order; the comparison does not treat them as identical texts.

[BOSON26](SOURCES.md#boson26) is a close conceptual comparison: its finite-signal analysis has quadratic residual-signal diffusion and explicitly suggests compensating drives. Appendices G.2 and I.2 distinguish the local experiment from the separate cost of global localization. The present proof supplies that type of global accounting for its own finite-dimensional Markovian model; it does not import the bosonic paper's fault-tolerance guarantees.

## Why the calibrated grid requires a physical-channel bound

[GLOBAL22](SOURCES.md#global22) establishes an inverse-use noise threshold for global Heisenberg scaling in phase-damping estimation. Quadratic mismatch loss combined with that threshold already predicts a field-resolution scale proportional to $`T^{-1/2}`$. This exponent alone is not a new general metrological principle.

The [grid upper bound](../science/grid_upper.md) continues estimating below the available spacing by combining fixed quadrature readouts with a calibrated classical phase projection. The [grid lower bound](../science/grid_lower.md) applies to the unrecovered physical cell and hence to arbitrary allowed boundary codes, ancillas, and adaptive sequences of grid fields. [RLD21](SOURCES.md#rld21) supplies the information chain rule; the midpoint prior and physical Choi bound apply it to the selectable family. The matched theorem assumes a grid gap inside the fixed parameter band, with $`0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\le W`$.

[INTERPOLATE17](SOURCES.md#interpolate17) shows why a hardware grid by itself does not imply an estimation floor: sequences of available propagators can produce finer effective control. Accordingly, the converse here includes switching and dithering between grid values at cell boundaries. It excludes subcell switching, a coherently controlled field register, and access to environmental records, as specified in the model.

## Public scientific antecedents in the same collection

[Noise-Rate-Activated-Sensing](SOURCES.md#noiserate), at the pinned revision in the source index, already contains the compensated telegraph-response calculation and weighted nested-interval estimation. Both ingredients are credited here. Its microscopic global result treats a controlled-rate, unknown-axis collision device with physical compensation held fixed. The present problem instead makes a calibrated physical counterfield selectable and asks when that resource supports global fixed-cadence precision.

[Quantum-Sensing-Without-Bath-Resets](SOURCES.md#bathreset) studies a finite inaccessible persistent environment and guarantees local inverse-time risk without resetting it. Its general implementation increases the correction rate with the budget; a native dephasing subclass has a separate fixed-cadence guarantee. That result neither classifies fixed-cadence Heisenberg scaling for the present memoryless semigroup nor supplies the compensation-grid tradeoff.

## Claim boundaries

The positive classification concerns existence of a sufficiently small cadence selected once, not every externally supplied cadence or every broad band at a fixed clock. Aliasing can prevent global identification. The general negative result is an $`\Omega(T^{-1})`$ lower bound, not a universal claim that SQL risk is attainable. The grid upper bound uses a known positive qubit jump rate; the unknown-rate general sufficiency result is a different statement.

These comparisons identify the mathematical ingredients and operational differences on which the repository relies. They do not certify exhaustive priority, universality beyond the stated noise class, or performance with imperfect controls.
